Magnetic Seed Amplification with Kinetic Recovery in Resistive Magnetohydrodynamics

Six mechanical forcing modes amplify a supplied oriented magnetic seed into a known field of fixed nonzero size while recovering small total velocity. The construction controls the coupled three-dimensional resistive MHD equations, with calibrated viscosity, uniform resistivity bounds and explicit preparation and resource costs.

The result

A prescribed fluid motion can stretch a magnetic field, but realizing that motion in a conducting fluid is harder: the magnetic field pushes back on the velocity. This paper constructs an ordinary mechanical forcing program that controls both effects and ends with the total velocity small.

With calibrated viscosity, six fixed forcing modes take a field close to a supplied oriented seed of size δ to an explicitly represented magnetic field of fixed order-one norm. The velocity is recovered near zero, and the magnetic norm stays bounded throughout. One prescribed force works for every positive resistivity in the stated bounded range, every admitted preparation error and absolute disturbance, and every energy-admissible Leray–Hopf realization. The output is chosen by the construction rather than being an arbitrary requested magnetic pattern.

How the argument works

The ideal stretching step builds on Rowan’s shear construction. An exact Fourier identity allows three deterministic phase candidates to replace a random phase choice for the known central seed. Repeating the selected steps grows a tracked magnetic observation; an offline stopping rule fixes the final magnetic size. The resulting field has an explicit shear-composition representation that can be evaluated to a requested accuracy.

A rapid forcing program realizes the ideal motion in the coupled equations. Comparing the velocity and magnetic errors together makes their leading nonlinear exchanges cancel, so the proof need not estimate the Lorentz force separately from magnetic energy. Relative-energy bounds then control the physical endpoint, while a finite-bit compiler pays for the force, timing, preparation radius and numerical precision. A weaker magnetic observation uses a backward induction test and tolerates unknown viscosity as well.

Scope

The main theorem requires a known oriented central seed, a paid preparation radius around zero velocity and that seed, and calibrated positive viscosity; the force is common over all positive resistivities bounded above by the stated normalization. Its force and synthesis bounds are polynomial in numerical inverse seed size and accuracy, with binary input costs included separately. Seed acquisition and viscosity calibration are not supplied, and absolute error tolerance is not fixed relative actuator accuracy.

The unknown-viscosity theorem guarantees a magnetic observation, without kinetic recovery or full-field accuracy. Near-guide preparation instead uses supplied nonzero flux and a growing actuator bank. Twelve-mode erasure and velocity steering are corollaries of the companion’s endpoint theorem, specialized to constant coefficients and zero magnetic mean. The further joint-target theorem with one signed magnetic channel remains conditional on its separate smooth-prefix implementation hypothesis. These are finite-time operations with large costs; sustained regulation, bounded-authority amplification and a physical actuator realization are not established. Mechanical forcing cannot create magnetism from zero magnetic input.

Technical abstract

We construct prescribed mechanical forces for the three-dimensional viscous, resistive magnetohydrodynamic equations on a torus. With calibrated viscosity, six fixed mechanical modes take a supplied magnetic seed of size δ and prepare an explicitly represented magnetic field of order one and recover small total velocity. The magnetic L² norm remains bounded throughout. The conclusion holds for every energy-admissible Leray–Hopf realization, uniformly over all positive magnetic diffusivities bounded above, with fixed absolute additive disturbances. A separate construction uses one force for all positive viscosities and diffusivities bounded above and all bounded initial velocities, but guarantees only a magnetic Fourier output. The proofs combine an elementary deterministic selection of ideal shear phases with quantitative relative-energy and duality estimates; numerical synthesis and physical authority have polynomial bounds in the inverse seed and accuracy. We also prove a near-guide-field preparation result with a growing actuator bank. We record common twelve-mode erasure and velocity steering as corollaries of a companion ensemble-control theorem, with constant coefficients and zero magnetic mean. A further joint-target construction with one repeatedly signed magnetic channel remains conditional on explicit smooth-magnetic-prefix implementation estimates. These operations have different preparations and control ports. The results concern costly finite-time endpoints; they do not provide bounded-authority amplification, sustained regulation, or a physical actuator realization.
