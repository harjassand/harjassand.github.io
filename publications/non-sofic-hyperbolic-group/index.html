An example of a non-sofic hyperbolic group

A negatively curved group can resist approximation by finite permutations. The paper constructs a torsion-free, two-dimensional hyperbolic group that is not sofic, linking geometric group theory with the limits of finite models.

Harjas Sandhu and Andreas Thom

17 October 2026 · Version 1

The result

A group is sofic when every finite part of its multiplication law can be modelled, to arbitrarily small error, by permutations of a finite set. The paper constructs a torsion-free word-hyperbolic group for which such approximations fail.

The group is the fundamental group of a finite two-dimensional triangle complex with a locally CAT(−1) metric. Its geometric description and obstruction to finite permutation models hold simultaneously, providing an explicit connection between negative curvature and failure of sofic approximation.

How the argument works

The construction uses marked lines in a finite incidence geometry to prescribe triangular relations. Polynomial interpolation and matrix-rank estimates force a uniform obstruction: any permutation assignment either violates a triangle relation on a positive fraction of points or makes a designated four-edge word fix too many points.

A large-girth incidence graph supports the negative-curvature metric and ensures that the designated words are nontrivial. These two alternatives are incompatible with arbitrarily accurate sofic approximations. The construction builds on the earlier non-residually finite hyperbolic-group construction credited in the paper.

Scope

The approximation notion is soficity: approximate multiplication and separation in finite symmetric groups, measured by normalized Hamming distance. Ruling out exact finite quotients alone would not establish this obstruction.

The theorem produces a specific finite presentation and a finite connected triangle complex. The geometric conclusion is torsion-free word hyperbolicity; the construction does not aim to minimize the presentation size.

Technical abstract

We construct a torsion-free, two-dimensional, word-hyperbolic group which is not sofic. Our construction builds on OpenAI's construction of a non-residually finite hyperbolic group, see OpenAI 252.
