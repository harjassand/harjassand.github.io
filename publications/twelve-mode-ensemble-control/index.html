Twelve-Mode Ensemble Control of Navier–Stokes and Resistive MHD

One prescribed force in twelve spatial modes prepares a chosen velocity and erases magnetic fluctuations across an uncertainty class of three-dimensional conducting fluids. The construction handles unknown time-dependent viscosity and resistivity, with explicit preparation, disturbance and synthesis bounds for finite-time endpoints.

The result

A conducting fluid has two coupled fields: its velocity and its magnetic field. Ensemble control asks for one forcing program that works before the initial fields and transport coefficients are known. Here the uncertainty class is an entire bounded H¹ preparation ball, which controls both the fields and their first spatial derivatives.

The theorem constructs one force in twelve fixed sine and cosine shear fields on the periodic unit torus. At a prescribed final time it prepares a chosen mean-zero, finite Fourier velocity target and brings the magnetic field close to its own conserved spatial mean in the joint L² norm. The same program works for every initial pair and every energy-admissible Leray–Hopf realization in the stated class; it never samples the actual coefficient functions. The Navier–Stokes case follows when the magnetic input and magnetic disturbance vanish.

How the argument works

The key reset distinguishes fluid perturbations from magnetic transport. A closed sequence of shears returns particle motion exactly, while the fluid pressure creates a cumulative contraction of velocity perturbations. Signed stirring cells then dissipate magnetic fluctuations, and interleaved resets control the velocity generated by magnetic feedback. The proof pays for fluctuations produced by a constant magnetic input as well as for nonconstant inputs.

After a waiting stage, a finite sequence of shear operations writes the target velocity. The analysis controls coefficient variation over each complete operation without measuring the coefficients. A deterministic compiler turns the program into an ordinary rational force with time polynomials of degree at most four. Coupled energy estimates and strong continuation for the designed horizon transfer the endpoint to every admitted weak realization.

Scope

Viscosity and resistivity may be independent unknown real Lipschitz functions, but their positive lower bounds, upper bounds and a common temporal Lipschitz bound must be supplied. The initial pair is real and divergence-free, the velocity has zero mean, and the target is an explicitly supplied rational Fourier polynomial. Disturbances are mean-zero absolute additive errors below the compiler’s tolerance; the theorem does not give fixed fractional actuator or timing robustness. The unknown magnetic mean is conserved.

Force bounds and inverse disturbance tolerance have fixed polynomial bounds in numerical preparation, inverse diffusivity, inverse time, inverse accuracy and temporal regularity parameters. Complete binary work, memory and output costs also include the target description length. This is not a polynomial bound in binary input length alone, and the universal finite precomputation can be extremely large. The theorem concerns costly endpoints under globally supported periodic forcing; arbitrary path tracking, zero diffusivity, economical hardware and a matched engineering advantage are not established.

Technical abstract

We construct a single prescribed momentum force for an uncertainty class of three-dimensional viscous, resistive magnetohydrodynamic equations on the unit torus. The viscosity and magnetic diffusivity are unknown independent Lipschitz functions, with supplied positive lower bounds and a supplied temporal Lipschitz bound. For every unknown initial pair in a joint H¹ ball, the same force prepares a chosen finite Fourier velocity target and returns the magnetic field close to its own unknown conserved mean. The conclusion holds for every admitted additive disturbance and every energy-admissible Leray–Hopf realization. The force uses exactly the twelve fundamental shear fields and ordinary rational time polynomials of degree at most four. A deterministic compiler has fixed polynomial numerical and complete binary resource bounds. The proof combines a concrete returning pressure-driven reset, coupled energy estimates, a full-space nonidentical-cell heat comparison and a depth-two target writer. Navier–Stokes and constant-coefficient cases are specializations. The results concern costly finite-time endpoints with globally supported periodic forcing, not path tracking, zero diffusivity or an economical physical actuator realization.
