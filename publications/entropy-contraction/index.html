Exact Reference-Assisted Entropy Contraction from the Choi Matrix

An exact finite optimization determines how much distinguishability a quantum channel can preserve in correlations with an untouched memory. The paper connects several entropy measures, separates ordinary mutual-information contraction, and shows why exact channel data and experimental estimation have different limits.

The result

A noisy quantum channel can erase differences between input states while leaving some information in correlations with a memory outside the channel. This paper asks for the largest fraction of distinguishability that can survive, allowing any finite quantum memory. It compares states with the same memory marginal, or subtracts the distinguishability already present in that memory.

For every finite-dimensional channel, one finite semidefinite program in its Choi matrix gives the exact answer. The complete, reference-subtracted and explicitly conditional-mutual-information coefficients coincide. The same optimization also governs the stated modular Petz divergences, each fixed Petz Rényi order between zero and one, and quantum Chernoff divergence.

Ordinary mutual information allows a quantum reference but has no separate conditioning memory. Its contraction is a different quantity. For a qubit depolarizing channel with shrinkage one half, the ordinary relative-entropy, ordinary mutual-information and complete entropy coefficients are respectively 1/4, 1/3 and 3/8. The paper determines the qubit formulas on the full completely positive parameter interval.

How the argument works

The proof first measures how far all output observables are from one common center, retaining their cross terms in a matrix Gram inequality. A Stinespring representation converts this into the Choi optimization. A polar-decomposition argument shows that its center can be chosen positive.

That center gives a signed observable map through an erasure channel. Two separately controlled orders of multiplication produce the universal entropy inequality. For the matching lower bound, a rare coherent memory sector carries a perturbation whose two local marginals vanish exactly. The proof takes the small-perturbation limit first, and only then makes that sector rare.

The matrix formula also yields an exact tensor-product law, a common-output-row criterion for strict contraction and an inverse-probability formula for orthogonal-unitary noise. The ordinary qubit mutual-information calculation uses a separate pinching argument obtained from a classical channel and a flagged unitary orbit.

Scope

The input is a finite channel description. The Choi matrix is unnormalized, and the inverse formulation includes an essential support condition even when a pseudoinverse is used. The optimization is finite in Hilbert-space dimension; its matrix size can still be exponential in physical qubit count. No uniformly conditioned or strongly polynomial numerical algorithm is asserted.

The regular modular Petz theorem requires a nonaffine operator-convex generator with finite endpoint value and recession slope. Umegaki entropy has a separate proof. Rényi results concern each fixed order strictly between zero and one, and generalized conditional information means an explicit difference of divergences. They do not cover optimized or sandwiched Rényi conditional information, all orders above one, or a limit uniform in the rare-sector weight.

For higher-dimensional depolarization the ordinary mutual-information coefficient has proved bounds and a strict separation from the complete coefficient, rather than an exact formula. At pure reset the complete coefficient is discontinuous, so no uniformly bounded number of adaptive black-box channel uses estimates it on every channel. Rare sharpness witnesses may carry vanishing absolute information. A signed observable lift is not a physical simulator or a general recovery operation.

Technical abstract

How much distinguishability can a quantum channel preserve when its input is correlated with an untouched quantum reference? We give an exact finite semidefinite program for the complete, equal-reference Umegaki contraction coefficient of every finite-dimensional channel. Its value also equals the reference-subtracted relative-entropy coefficient and the conditional mutual-information coefficient. The program uses the unnormalized Choi matrix; its inverse formulation requires an explicit range condition. The proof identifies a centered complete Gram optimization, constructs a state-independent signed observable lift through an erasure channel, and proves sharpness with a rare coherent reference sector. References of bounded dimension suffice, even when both input marginals are prescribed and faithful. The same program computes the corresponding coefficients for regular modular Petz divergences, each fixed Petz Rényi order between zero and one, and quantum Chernoff divergence. Consequences include a harmonic dual, exact tensor products, a sharp comparison with positive CP-replacer minorization, and explicit classical and unitary-noise formulas. We separately compute ordinary quantum mutual-information contraction for qubit depolarization, which is strictly smaller. A discontinuity at pure reset rules out uniformly bounded-use black-box estimation, distinguishing exact Choi-data evaluation from robust acquisition of an unknown channel.
