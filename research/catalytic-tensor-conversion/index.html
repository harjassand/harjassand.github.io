Fixed-Format Undecidability of Exact Catalytic Tensor Restriction

Abstract

We present a complete AI-generated proof candidate for a universal representation compiler from finite-dimensional unital representations of finitely presented associative algebras to exact stochastic three-tensor conversion with an exactly returned bipartite maximally entangled catalyst. The construction uses rank-drop hyperplanes to force unrestricted first-party maps to preserve labels and a full-rank marker slice to isolate the encoded algebra. Its principal claimed consequence is an RE-complete catalytic conversion problem along one fixed rank-one affine line of strictly positive integer tensors. A two-dimensional Pauli-representation witness shows genuine exact catalysis within the construction. This is an exact mathematical claim, not a new quantum device.

Principal theorem claims

Representation compiler. For every finitely presented rational associative algebra A, the source constructs integer three-tensors TA and SA such that, for every positive integer d, exact conversion after adjoining a maximally entangled rank-d catalyst occurs if and only if A admits a unital d-dimensional complex matrix representation.

Fixed-affine undecidability. The source constructs fixed positive integer tensors U, V and S, with tensor rank one for V, such that deciding whether U + nV can be converted to S with an exactly returned catalyst is recursively enumerable complete as n varies.

Consequence. Under the same exact-conversion assumptions, no computable general bound on the necessary catalyst dimension exists. A fixed real affine family of convertible parameters is therefore not semialgebraic.

Why the construction is significant

The central obstruction in a universality proof is that general tensor-restriction maps are allowed to change all three tensor factors. Merely writing algebraic equations as matrix slices does not force these maps to respect the equations. The candidate proof adds families of labelled rank-deficiency hyperplanes with successively separated multiplicities. Rank monotonicity then prevents the first-party map from permuting the labels. A distinguished marker slice similarly prevents the remaining maps from hiding an invalid algebra representation inside auxiliary coordinates.

For the undecidability step, ordinary finite-dimensional representation theory encodes nonzero integers as central scalars of elementary matrix algebras. A universal Diophantine equation is inserted through a single varying relation coefficient. The tensor compiler transfers this dependence into one rank-one affine direction while retaining a fixed tensor format. An invertible basis transformation makes all coefficients positive.

The complete source-derived paper gives the equations, rank argument, representation reduction, integer gadget, positivity construction, finite example, and the precise limits of the claim.

What the theorem does not say

The catalyst is returned on a successful stochastic branch, whose probability may be arbitrarily small. The result is not about catalytic conversion at fixed nonzero error, finite precision, guaranteed success probability, deterministic LOCC, experimentally available catalyst dimensions, or the ordinary matrix-multiplication exponent. In particular, mathematical undecidability over an unbounded catalyst dimension does not establish any physically achievable hypercomputation.
