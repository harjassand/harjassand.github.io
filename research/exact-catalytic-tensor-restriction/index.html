Fixed-format undecidability of exact catalytic tensor restriction

A catalyst can enable an exact quantum-state conversion while being returned unchanged. A proposed tensor construction connects this effect to algebra representations and an undecidable conversion problem within one fixed positive tensor family.

Research direction and coordination: Harjas Sandhu

The result

The manuscript proposes a compiler from finitely presented rational associative algebras to three-party tensors. At each positive integer d, conversion with an exactly returned rank-d bipartite catalyst is equivalent to the existence of a unital d-dimensional complex matrix representation of the algebra.

The proposed reduction places an undecidable conversion problem along a single fixed rank-one affine family of strictly positive integer tensors. A small algebra with two anticommuting involutions supplies a concrete example: a two-dimensional representation exists although no scalar representation does, giving a proposed witness of genuine catalysis.

How the argument works

The challenge is to force arbitrary local tensor maps to respect the algebraic equations encoded in their matrix slices. The construction adds labelled blocks whose ranks drop on prescribed hyperplanes. These rank patterns are intended to pin down slice mixing, while a full-rank marker prevents auxiliary coordinates from concealing an invalid representation.

A representation-theoretic integer encoding and a universal Diophantine equation then transfer a computation problem into one varying tensor coefficient. A fixed invertible change of basis makes the tensors strictly positive.

Scope

The model is exact stochastic tensor restriction over the complex numbers. The catalyst is bipartite, its dimension is unbounded, and it is returned exactly and uncorrelated with the target on the successful branch. Success probability has no uniform positive lower bound.

The representation compiler and its consequences are presented as a proposed construction. Approximate return, deterministic local operations and a fixed positive success-rate guarantee require separate results.

Technical abstract

A proposed representation compiler links finite-dimensional representations of finitely presented rational associative algebras to exact stochastic restriction of three-tensors with an exactly returned bipartite maximally entangled catalyst. Rank-drop markers constrain otherwise unrestricted local maps. The resulting construction gives a route to recursively enumerable complete conversion along a fixed rank-one affine family of strictly positive integer tensors, together with a finite example of genuine catalysis. The model concerns exact conversion with unbounded catalyst dimension; it imposes no uniform positive success probability.

Further explanation and proof details

1. Exact conversion contract

Over the complex numbers, write a three-party tensor as a matrix pencil T = Σᵢ eᵢ ⊗ Aᵢ. Tensor restriction T ≽ S allows one linear map on each party, without requiring those maps to be invertible. For a positive integer d define its bipartite maximally entangled amplification:

T[d] = Σᵢ eᵢ ⊗ (Aᵢ ⊗ I_d)
T ≽cat S   ⇔   there exists d ≥ 1 such that T[d] ≽ S[d].

The auxiliary state is shared only between parties two and three and is returned exactly, uncorrelated with the target, on the successful branch. The probability of success may be arbitrarily small. No approximate return, deterministic LOCC guarantee or three-party auxiliary state is assumed.

2. Main proposed theorems

Representation universality

From any finite rational presentation 𝒜 = ℚ⟨X₁,…,Xₜ⟩/(p₁,…,pₛ), the construction effectively produces integer tensors T(𝒜) and S(𝒜) for which, at every catalyst dimension d,

T(𝒜)[d] ≽ S(𝒜)[d]
     ⇔  𝒜 admits a unital representation in M_d(ℂ).

The representation need not be faithful. Arithmetic-circuit relations can be converted to quadratic relations without changing which finite-dimensional representations exist.

Fixed positive rank-one affine undecidability

There exist fixed strictly positive integer tensors U, V and S, with U and V having a common format and tensor rank(V) = 1, such that the integer set

{ n ≥ 0 : U + nV ≽cat S }

is recursively enumerable complete. Thus each finite-dimensional instance is algebraic and decidable, but permitting an unknown, arbitrarily large catalyst makes the overall exact-conversion decision undecidable.

3. Construction and proof

3.1 Matrix representations as two-sided equations

Quadraticise the presentation and clear rational denominators. With X₀ = I, write each relation as a sum of terms cₐᵦXₐXᵦ. Introduce matrix units Eₐᵦ and equations P(Mᵢ ⊗ I_d)Q = bᵢI_d, where P and Q consist of unknown d-by-d blocks. The normalization M₀ = E₀₀ forces P₀Q₀ = I. Additional Eₐ₀ − E₀ₐ relations identify the variables P₀⁻¹Pₐ and QₐP₀. Every algebra relation then becomes a two-sided equation, and the construction is reversible.

3.2 Rank-drop markers prevent slice mixing

The difficult step is that arbitrary restriction maps can mix pencil slices. For every index i other than a distinguished zero slice *, adjoin the linear forms x* + xᵢ and 2x* + xᵢ, together with x* itself. Give the successive marker blocks strictly increasing sizes hⱼ = (r+1)j, where r is the number of core block variables.

D(x) = direct sum over j of [ℓⱼ(x) I_(hⱼ)]
A(x) = core-source(x) ⊕ D(x)
B(x) = core-target(x) ⊕ D(x).

The target pencil is concise in its first factor. Any first-factor map G in a restriction must be invertible. On a marker hyperplane ker ℓⱼ, the source rank falls by at least hⱼ − r. Rank monotonicity forces G to permute these hyperplanes. Since adjacent block sizes differ by r+1, the permutation can only be the identity. The labels ℓ = x*, x*+xᵢ and 2x*+xᵢ then force all dual eigenvalues to agree, so G is scalar. This is the key proposed new mechanism.

3.3 Saturating the marker block prevents a false representation

At the distinguished slice *, the core vanishes but every marker block is invertible. Its output has the full marker rank Kd, leaving no freedom for marker coordinates to contribute to the target core. Compressing all other slice equations to the output core therefore recovers exactly P(Mᵢ ⊗ I_d)Q = bᵢI_d. Conversely, any solution of these equations extends to a tensor restriction by identity maps on the marker blocks. This completes the representation compiler, provided the block-rigidity reasoning is correct.

3.4 Integrality and the halting reduction

Use the ordinary finite-dimensional representation theory of sl₂ and a central generator z constrained by z² = (h+1)² + 4fe. On every simple finite-dimensional representation, z takes a nonzero integer scalar value, and every such integer is attainable. Differences of two such scalars encode arbitrary integer variables. The classical Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem then provides one fixed polynomial P(n,x) encoding an recursively enumerable complete set.

Make n enter only through the relation t − nI = 0. After quadraticisation, the number of variables and relations is fixed; a single source coefficient is affine in n. The representation compiler consequently produces one fixed target and one rank-one source direction V.

3.5 Strictly positive coefficients

A fixed change of basis selects one corner contraction of both U and V equal to 1. For a fixed integer tensor W with corner coefficient 1 and all coefficients bounded in magnitude by M, apply to every factor the invertible map G = I + L·1·e₀ᵀ with L = 8M + 1. Every transformed coefficient equals L³ + ε with |ε| ≤ M(3L²+3L+1) < L³. Thus U, V and S may be taken strictly positive without changing their conversion relation or tensor rank(V).

3.6 A concrete witness to genuine catalysis

The relations X² = Y² = I and XY + YX = 0 have no scalar complex solution, but do have a two-dimensional solution given by Pauli matrices. The claimed compiler gives a 7×367×367 source and a 7×365×365 target that are not convertible without a catalyst but are convertible with d=2. This is an exact algebraic witness, not an experimental success-rate claim.

4. Consequences and research frontiers

The proposed fixed-format undecidability excludes every computable bound on the necessary catalyst dimension as a function of input size. It also excludes a sound, effectively enumerable, complete certificate family for all impossible exact conversions. Restricting U+nV to real parameters gives a nonsemialgebraic conversion set; otherwise membership on sufficiently large integers would stabilise, contradicting the halting encoding.

The decisive research extension is to determine whether any such obstruction survives physically meaningful approximation, bounded success probability and chargeable catalyst preparation. The exact theorem by itself neither demonstrates a quantum computational speedup nor implies that practical converters require unbounded resources. An approximation theorem or an effective fixed-noise cutoff would be a fundamentally different result.

5. Comparison and limitations

Known stochastic multipartite entanglement catalysis is not new. This proof candidate should be compared with Chen et al. (2010), Christandl, Gesmundo, Lysikov and Steffan, and Steffan's 2023 thesis. The claimed contribution is the rank-drop representation compiler and its fixed-format decidability consequences, not the existence of quantum catalysis or the classical undecidability of Diophantine equations.

No result is claimed for approximate conversion, a dimension-independent lower bound on success probability, deterministic LOCC, a full three-party catalyst, or the matrix multiplication exponent.
