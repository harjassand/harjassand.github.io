Thermal balance and the amplifier entropy photon-number inequality

Quantum amplification adds unavoidable noise. A variational argument seeks the sharp lower bound on output entropy for two independent inputs, even when the modes within either input are correlated.

Research direction and coordination: Harjas Sandhu

The result

The entropy photon number expresses a state’s entropy as the occupation of a thermal state with the same entropy per mode. The manuscript presents a variational argument for its sharp amplifier lower bound: at gain G, the output entropy photon number is at least G times the first input’s value plus (G − 1) times the sum of the second input’s value and one.

Thermal inputs attain the bound. For example, gain 2 and thermal occupations 1 and 0 give output occupation 3. The proposed inequality says that independent inputs with those same entropies cannot produce a smaller output entropy photon number.

How the argument works

The amplifier’s second input enters through a creation operator. That reversal changes the sign structure of a logarithmic-mean comparison and introduces the extra quantum-noise term. Thermal relaxation obeys a matching signed balance.

At a regularized entropy minimum, the two structures cancel the energy corrections. A thermal Stein estimate is then used to turn small balance defects into trace-norm closeness to thermal states, with finite-energy estimates closing the proposed argument. The variational framework and four-weight interpolation build on the earlier beam-splitter argument.

Scope

The general argument concerns two independent finite-energy input groups with any fixed finite number of modes. Correlations and entanglement within either group are allowed. Correlations between the two groups can violate the independent-input formula.

The manuscript also studies Gaussian spectral refinements and Gaussian equality. General non-Gaussian equality and a quantitative stability theorem remain open. The proposed general amplifier argument and its Gaussian special cases should be read at their respective scopes.

Technical abstract

The entropy photon number is the occupation of a thermal state with a given entropy per mode. We give a variational argument for its sharp lower bound under amplification of two independent finite-energy inputs, allowing correlations within each input group. The proof turns on an agreement of signs: the creation port reverses a logarithmic-mean metric, and thermal relaxation obeys the corresponding signed occupation balance. At a regularized entropy minimum, these two facts cancel the energy corrections and force the thermal-balance defects to vanish. A trace-norm Stein bound, obtained from thermal-attenuator smoothing, then closes the argument. We also give a Gaussian spectral refinement, characterize Gaussian equality by a common symplectic shape, and deduce a sharp correlation bound for the two outputs. The conjecture is due to De Palma, Mari and Giovannetti; the variational framework and four-weight interpolation are adapted from OpenAI's beam-splitter argument.

Further explanation and proof details

Reader’s explanation

What an amplifier cannot leave behind

A thermal calculation is easy. The question is whether any independent quantum inputs with the same entropies can do better.

Feed vacuum into both ports of an amplifier with gain G > 1. The output you keep is thermal, with G − 1 photons per mode. The pump supplies the energy. The unavoidable extra contribution comes from the quantum commutation relation.

Simply multiplying a mode’s annihilation operator by √G would multiply its commutator by G. A physical amplifier needs a second mode, entering through a creation operator, to restore the correct commutator. That reversal brings in the identity bb† = b†b + 1. The extra unit survives even when that second input is vacuum.

The amplifier is unitary on its two outputs together: it preserves their joint entropy. The thermal entropy appears in the retained output because the other output is discarded. The distinction between a pure joint state and a mixed marginal is essential.

Put entropy on a thermal scale

A one-mode thermal state with mean occupation r has entropy g(r) = (r + 1) log(r + 1) − r log r, with g(0) = 0. All logarithms here are natural.

For an n-mode state, ask which thermal occupation would give the same entropy per mode. The answer is its entropy photon number, 𝒩n(ρ) = g−1(S(ρ)/n). This measures uncertainty on a thermal scale; it is not the state’s actual mean energy.

Write ΓG(ρA, ρB) for the retained amplifier output. The manuscript proposes the sharp inequality

𝒩n(ΓG(ρA,ρB))≥G𝒩n(ρA)+(G−1)[𝒩n(ρB)+1].

The claim concerns every fixed finite number of modes and finite-energy inputs. The two input groups must be independent, although the modes within either group may be correlated or entangled. Thermal inputs attain equality: their occupations combine as Ga + (G − 1)(b + 1).

For example, at gain 2, thermal inputs with occupations 1 and 0 give output occupation 3. The proposed bound says that replacing those inputs by arbitrary independent states with the same entropies cannot lower the output’s entropy photon number below 3.

Independence cannot be weakened to separability. Section 9 gives classically correlated coherent inputs whose displacements cancel at the output and defeat the independent-input formula.

Where the two signs meet

The proposed proof approaches the bound from two directions. One is geometric: the curvature of entropy gives a comparison involving logarithmic means. Four-weight interpolation turns that comparison into a thermal metric. The creation port reverses the order of the metric’s two thermal rates.

The other direction is dynamical. Relax each input toward a thermal reference. For the amplifier to carry these relaxations into the matching output relaxation, the reference occupations must obey the same signed balance, R = Ga + (G − 1)(b + 1). The extra unit and the reversed orientation have appeared again.

The point is not merely that the formulas resemble one another. At a regularized minimum of a putative entropy deficit, the two structures are paired in the stationary equations. Their energy corrections cancel exactly. Equation (6.5) is the meeting point: interpolation bounds the remaining output defect, while strict comparison slack gives the input defects a larger opposing coefficient.

The argument then forces those defects to vanish as the moment penalty is removed. A thermal Stein estimate turns small balance residuals into closeness to the thermal states in trace norm. Uniform energy bounds supply the final entropy-continuity step. The minimizing states return to the thermal calculation, contradicting the negative deficit from which the argument began.

This agreement between entropy geometry and thermal dynamics is the organizing idea of the proof candidate. Sections 3–7 and Appendices A–D give the complete argument and its domain estimates.

Contribution and prior work

The inequality is the amplifier conjecture of De Palma, Mari and Giovannetti. The manuscript’s claim is a proof for two arbitrary independent finite-energy multimode inputs, extending beyond one-mode Gaussian-channel optimizer results and restricted multimode cases.

The regularized variational framework and four-weight interpolation are adapted from OpenAI’s beam-splitter proof. The active-port analysis, its matching relaxation balance, stationary cancellation and finite-energy closure are the amplifier-specific parts presented here. The full adapted interpolation argument is retained in Appendix C.

The Gaussian-input inequality itself is established prior work. Section 8 gives a spectral refinement and a separate equality argument using Bhatia–Jain’s symplectic variational principle. Within the Gaussian class, equality requires flat symplectic spectra in each input group and a common normalized covariance shape after reversing the second input’s momentum quadratures. Displacements remain free.

The trace-norm Stein estimate in Section 7 builds on Gao–Rouzé’s thermal beam-splitter smoothing mechanism. Section 9 derives a sharp two-output correlation bound conditional on the general amplifier inequality. General non-Gaussian equality and a quantitative EPnI stability estimate remain open; the Stein estimate alone does not settle them. Priority for the refinements is not established by this release.
