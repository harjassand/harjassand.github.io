Mathematical foundations for new capabilities

My aim is to contribute to a deeper understanding of the world and to the foundations of new technology. The papers collected here explore mathematical questions in computation, information and the control of physical systems.

I hope these results can be examined, connected and built on by researchers and engineers, leading to new theories, methods and applications.

Controlling conducting fluids

From a common forcing program to a response that depends on an unknown magnetic mean. The seed-preparation paper studies a distinct operation; its erasure corollaries use the common-control theorem.

  1. Common control

    One twelve-mode force prepares a chosen velocity and erases magnetic fluctuations across the stated uncertainty class.

  2. Nonlinear response

    Uses the quantitative common-control foundation to program a joint polynomial response to the unknown conserved magnetic mean.

  3. Magnetic preparation

    Amplifies a supplied oriented seed while recovering small velocity. Its direct preparation construction has a different input contract.

Prediction and protection under quantum noise

Three complementary questions: what can be predicted, what can be retained, and how noise constrains output entropy. Each paper uses its own physical model and assumptions.

  1. Predicting readouts

    Local noise permits classical prediction of specified global observations in driven two-dimensional quantum systems.

  2. Protecting information

    Storage bounds use thermally rare error boundaries and explicit preparation and access assumptions.

  3. Entropy of amplification

    A proposed variational argument addresses output entropy for quantum amplifiers; Gaussian results retain their separate scope.

Comparing quantum information

Complete channel comparison leads to capacity and distillation consequences. Maximal-information profiles examine a different invariant and show why agreement of tests need not imply physical simulation.

  1. Complete information order

    Relates information comparisons with arbitrary finite references and establishes undecidability at the stated exact scope.

  2. Communication consequences

    Uses the companion order results to derive exact-zero capacity and one-way distillation obstructions.

  3. Maximal-information profiles

    Studies RLD profiles and canonical realizations. Equality of these profiles is distinct from the complete order used in the companion pair.

Amplifying tests through repetition

Entropy accounting controls correlated test outcomes. Applying it to a game also requires a reconstruction argument; the projection-game paper supplies a result for its specified predicate class.

  1. Entropy and concentration

    Develops weighted concentration and identifies reconstruction barriers; its commuting-game applications retain a reconstruction hypothesis.

  2. Projection games and communication

    Proves quadratic repetition for commuting projection games and charges a specified budget of noisy quantum communication.

Limits of computation and finite description

These papers investigate three different boundaries: deciding an equilibrium phase, approximating a group by finite permutations, and exact catalytic conversion. They share a question about finite descriptions, rather than a single construction.

  1. Equilibrium uniqueness

    A fixed-temperature spin family encodes halting, while its unique-equilibrium branch has exponentially decaying correlations.

  2. Finite group models

    Constructs a hyperbolic group that cannot be approximated by finite permutations in the sense of soficity.

  3. Catalytic conversion

    A proposed representation compiler links exact tensor restriction with an undecidable conversion problem. Its construction status is retained.
